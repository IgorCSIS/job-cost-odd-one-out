What does not match

Hit Find the odd one. and the reason shows up here, in words.

A spread is how far a job usually sits from the middle on that line, so a line at 12 spreads is twelve times the normal wobble.

How this is figured
  1. Scale the columns. Hours, dollars and minutes cannot be compared as they are, so each column is measured against its own middle value and its own typical distance from that middle. The middle rather than the average, because an average is dragged toward whatever is unusual, and the unusual thing is what we are looking for.
  2. Drop the duplicate direction. If two columns turn out to be one number recorded twice, they are folded into one. Keeping both counts that spend twice and makes the whole book look like it moves together.
  3. Flag the point farthest from the normal cluster. With the columns on the same footing and the duplicate gone, measure how far each job sits from the middle of the pack, and report the farthest one plus whichever line put it there.

That is linear algebra on a small table, not a forecast. It does not learn, it has no opinion about what a job should cost, and it cannot tell a typo from a real equipment run. It only says which line does not match the others. Totals are left out of the arithmetic on purpose: a total is labor plus materials plus permits, so scoring it would count those three a second time and the biggest job would always win.
